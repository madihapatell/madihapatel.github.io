During my internship I wrote for two beauty brands at once, a skincare line and a makeup line. In the same week I'd write long blog posts and copy for product category and collection pages. Both were "SEO content," but they had almost nothing else in common.

That's the lesson I keep coming back to: **a blog post and a category page can target related keywords, but they're answering completely different questions.**

#### Start with search intent

Before writing anything, ask what the person searching is trying to do.

- **Informational intent:** "how to build a skincare routine", "what is niacinamide used for". They want to *learn*. A blog post fits.
- **Commercial or transactional intent:** "niacinamide serum", "matte lipstick shades". They want to *browse or buy*. A category or collection page fits.

If your page type doesn't match the intent, it's an uphill battle no matter how good the writing is.

#### Writing for category and collection pages

Category pages exist to help people choose and buy. The copy should support the products, not bury them.

- **Keep the top short.** One or two lines under the heading that say what's in the collection and who it's for. Shoppers want to see products, not paragraphs.
- **Use the main keyword naturally** in the H1 and the intro, written the way people actually search for it.
- **Add depth further down.** A short section below the product grid can cover how to choose, key ingredients or finishes, and common questions. It helps search engines and doesn't get in the shopper's way.
- **Link sideways and down.** Point to related categories and to blog posts that answer the bigger "how" questions.

#### Writing SEO blogs

Blogs exist to answer a question properly, and to earn the trust that eventually leads someone to the products.

- **Answer the question early.** Then go deeper with steps, examples and comparisons.
- **Structure for scanning** with clear subheadings, short paragraphs and lists where they help.
- **Link to the right category pages** where it's genuinely useful, not in every paragraph.
- **Work with design.** A good banner and in-blog creatives make long content easier to read. I always try to brief these alongside the copy.

#### Don't make your pages compete

The easiest mistake is writing a blog post that targets the *exact same* keyword as a category page. Then the two pages compete with each other in search. Give each page its own primary keyword and its own job, and connect them with internal links.

#### The short version

| | Blog post | Category page |
|---|---|---|
| The searcher wants to… | Learn | Browse or buy |
| Copy length | Long, in-depth | Short on top, optional depth below |
| Main goal | Answer the question, build trust | Help them choose a product |
| Links to | Relevant categories and products | Related categories and helpful guides |

Same keyword family, different jobs. Get the job right and the writing gets a lot easier.
