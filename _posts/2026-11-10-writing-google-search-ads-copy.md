Writing Google Search Ads copy for a home-furnishing brand taught me one thing fast: thirty characters is *not* a lot of room.

Here's what I've learned about writing search ads that fit the limits and still sound like the brand.

#### Know your limits (literally)

Responsive Search Ads give you a few building blocks, each with a hard character cap:

| Element | Character limit | How many |
|---|---|---|
| Headline | 30 | Up to 15 |
| Description | 90 | Up to 4 |
| Sitelink text | 25 | Several, at account/campaign/ad group level |
| Sitelink description lines | 35 each | 2 per sitelink |
| Callout | 25 | Several |

Google mixes and matches your headlines and descriptions. You usually see 2 or 3 headlines and up to 2 descriptions at once, so **every headline has to make sense next to any other headline.**

#### 1. Write headlines that work in any order

Since Google decides the combinations, avoid headlines that depend on each other ("Our new collection…" followed by "…is finally here"). Each one should stand alone.

A good mix covers different angles:

- **The keyword:** what the person actually searched for
- **The benefit:** why it matters to them
- **The proof:** experience, reviews, guarantees, in-house manufacturing
- **The offer:** if there's a real one
- **The action:** shop, book a visit, get a quote

#### 2. Put the search term in at least a couple of headlines

People scan ads for the words they typed. If someone searches "modular wardrobe Mumbai", a headline that mirrors that wording feels like the right answer before they've read anything else.

#### 3. Use descriptions to finish the thought

Ninety characters is enough for one clear idea and one call to action. Lead with the benefit and end with what to do next. Don't repeat your headlines word for word; you're paying for that space.

#### 4. Callouts are for facts, sitelinks are for destinations

- **Callouts** are short, scannable facts, like delivery and installation, warranty, or showroom visits. Think "quick reasons to trust us."
- **Sitelinks** should send people to *real* pages they might want, like a category, a store locator or a collection. A sitelink to the homepage wastes a slot.

#### 5. Pin sparingly

You can pin a headline to position 1 (for example, the brand name), but every pin reduces the combinations Google can test. I only pin when a brand or legal requirement means it has to be there.

#### 6. Keep it on-brand, even when it's short

Short copy is where brand voice usually disappears. Read your headlines out loud. If one could belong to any competitor, rewrite it.

#### The takeaway

Good search ads copy isn't about squeezing in every selling point. Pick the few things this searcher needs to hear, say each one cleanly within the limit, and give the system enough good variations to work with.
