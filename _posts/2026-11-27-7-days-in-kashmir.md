This is my first-hand 7-day itinerary through Srinagar, Sonmarg, Gulmarg, and Pahalgam. It includes the food I ate and the mistakes that I made but that you can avoid.

We landed in Srinagar at 11 in the morning. The flight had already started the fun for us. We flew Mumbai-Delhi-Srinagar, and I had gotten the right-hand-side window for myself. It's important because from the right, the Himalayas appear as close as they can at an aerial view.

This is the 7-day Kashmir itinerary I wish I had found before we left. I mention how each day felt, what you should order, what we missed, and what I would do differently.

## The 7-Day Kashmir Itinerary At A Glance

- Route: Srinagar (3 nights, one of which on a houseboat) → Sonmarg → Gulmarg → Pahalgam → Katra.
- How long: 7 days and 6 nights.
- When to visit: Early to mid-October for falling chinar leaves and late November for early snow.
- Book ahead: Gondola rides (both phases), a joyride at Gulmarg, and a riverside table at Cafe Wilo.

## Day 1: Wazwan, A French-Looking Bakery, And Golden Hour On Dal Lake

We went to Stream restaurant on Boulevard Road first thing after checking into our hotel and ordered the wazwan thali. It arrived as a rice dish topped with seekh kebab and lamb and five gravies, including the rogan gosh, mutton chilli korma, Kashmiri waza paneer, mutton rista, and mutton gushtaba.

![Wazwan thali with rice, seekh kebab at Stream Restaurant, Srinagar]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/02-wazwan-thali-stream-restaurant.webp){: loading="lazy"}

The meat was chewy, a texture that is expected, not a fault. The gravies had a spiced, fresh flavor that made us keep taking servings. I liked the rogan gosh and gushtaba best. The gushtaba is a mutton meatball in a yogurt-based gravy, and it's something I would order again in an instant.

After lunch we walked along Dal Lake to Ghat No. 9, where Le Delice Bakery sits, looking like a French street side. The bakes drew us in, and we started with the cheesecakes. We tried the Basque cheesecake, chocolate brownie, red velvet cheesecake, butterscotch cake, and walnut brownie.

![A display of freshly baked cheesecakes, brownies, and pastries at Le Delice Bakery in Srinagar, Kashmir.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/03-le-delice-bakery-display.webp){: loading="lazy"}

By the time we finished, it was golden hour. We sat by the lake and watched the light go and the air turn cold. After dark the market filled up, so we took a stroll and then went back to the hotel.

## Day 2: Lake, Hazratbal, and Food Runs

![A beautiful autumn chinar leaf at the garden near Manasbal Lake, Kashmir.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/04-chinar-leaf-manasbal-lake.webp){: loading="lazy"}

We started early at Manasbal Lake and spent a long time admiring chinar leaves at the garden.

At one end of the garden is a small food shop with a tiny menu - Chinese, pizza, and momo. We ordered the pizza. It had a thin, crisp crust with generous saucy toppings. It was genuinely delicious. The two men running the counter were kind and friendly.

Next we visited Hazratbal Dargah. The interiors are made of wood and marble. The center area with high ceilings, tinted glass, and a beautiful hanging piece is mesmerizing. But the inner shrine is accessible only to men. On the other side, a wide veranda opens up to the lake. It is a completely calm place to slow down.

![The wooden and marble interiors of Hazratbal Dargah, featuring high ceilings, tinted glass windows, and an ornate hanging centrepiece.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/05-hazratbal-dargah-interior.webp){: loading="lazy"}

Near the dargah are the two bakeries everyone mentions - Moonlight and New Taj. At Moonlight we tried the long-awaited walnut fudge. It was fine, a 6/10 for me. At New Taj, the cream roll we got was flaky and just sweet enough. The walnut biscuits were soft and stole a tiny space in our luggage too.

For dinner we went to one of the trout stalls near Ghat No. 13. The fish comes with two rotis and four kinds of chutneys. It's grilled over an open flame, which adds the smoky flavor to it. The flesh was soft and melted in the mouth. If you eat one thing from a stall in Srinagar, let it be this. We also tried pani puri at the lake-side stalls, but our Mumbaikar taste buds weren't satisfied.

![Whole grilled trout served with rotis and four chutneys at a Ghat 13 stall, Dal Lake]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/06-grilled-trout-ghat-13-dal-lake.webp){: loading="lazy"}

## Day 3: Harissa, Flower Gardens, and Living By The Lake

We checked out of our hotel and went straight to Zafrani Harissa. Harissa is a mutton dish that is cooked through the night and served early in the morning. So you have to wake up early and visit or it'll be over by 9 am. The meat was tender enough to melt in your mouth, and the girda served with it was soft.

![A traditional Kashmiri breakfast of slow-cooked mutton harissa served with soft, freshly baked girda bread at Zafrani Harissa, Srinagar.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/07-mutton-harissa-girda-zafrani-harissa.webp){: loading="lazy"}

Then it was time for strolling through gardens - Bagh-e-Gul-e-Dawood, Pari Mahal, and Chashme Shahi.

Bagh-e-Gul-e-Dawoood is the new chrysanthemum show inside the Nehru Memorial Botanical Garden. Since it opened on 23 October 2026, we had the chance to visit the place. It brings a bright and cheerful mood to the autumn season of Kashmir. Colorful flowers with the Zabarwan Hills in the backdrop is a beautiful view to witness.

A practical thing to note is that private vehicles cannot take you up to the gardens, so you have to take a union cab from the stands near Nehru Garden. Do not agree on the first price quoted. We bargained for a 7-person cab down to ₹2000 for the circuit.

![The traditional wooden interiors of a Dal Lake houseboat, featuring intricately carved ceilings and cosy Kashmiri-style alcoves.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/08-dal-lake-houseboat-interior.webp){: loading="lazy"}

It was a sunny day, so we stopped exploring around 4 and went back to Dal Lake to check into our houseboat. The interiors were made of wood, and the carved ceilings, little alcoves, and pinterest-worthy corners added the old Kashmir vibes. Just a little before sunset, we took a shikara ride. The moment you start sailing, other shikaras surround you to sell fruit, chaat, jewelry, kahwa, and whatnot. You have to say no firmly and repeatedly to shoo them away and get some quiet. Do the Kashmiri pheran photoshoot, though. It takes 15 minutes and is worth it for the memories.

Our last stop in Srinagar was Chai Jaai. We reached there at 7 pm, and it was packed. We ordered noon chai, a pink, salt-based tea that is oddly comforting with sheermal and fries. Go earlier during the day for the pretty sunlight over the crockery photos.

![Beautifully arranged Kashmiri crockery at Chai Jaai Cafe in Srinagar, where traditional noon chai is served.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/09-kashmiri-crockery-chai-jaai.webp){: loading="lazy"}

## Day 4: Sonmarg, Zero Point

![A breathtaking view of snow-covered Himalayan mountain peaks from Zojila Point near Sonmarg, Kashmir.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/10-zojila-point-snow-peaks-sonmarg.webp){: loading="lazy"}

We left for Sonmarg, and the views changed - snow-capped mountains, tall trees, and a teal-blue river running alongside the road.

To reach zero point, we took a union cab. At the top near Zojila, it was so cold that taking gloves off was also a challenge. We ate maggi and drank chai and warmed our hands at a bonfire.

Then I tried to trek up the mountain to where there was snow in my boot heels. But the path was steep, and I realized the boots were a mistake. We also got to see a small waterfall at the point. Higher up was the war memorial site.

The drive back got the best of us. Traffic made the way back 2 hours long, and it was pitch black by 6 pm. We reached our hotel at 10, ate dinner, and went straight to bed.

## Day 5: Gulmarg’s Long Queue

![A view of other cable cars suspended over the snow-covered Himalayan mountains during the Gulmarg Gondola ride.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/11-gulmarg-gondola-cable-cars.webp){: loading="lazy"}

Gulmarg is known for its gondola ride, and the gondola ride is known for its never-ending queue. And the rumors are true. The line starts on the ground floor and continues up to the first. And it's not a straight line! However, it's worth it.

There are two phases. Phase 1 takes you to an open field with the snow mountains arranged in the view. Phase 2 goes higher up to Apharwat. The day we went, phase 2 was closed due to technical reasons.

![The snowy mountain views at Gulmarg Gondola Phase 1 in Kashmir.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/12-gulmarg-gondola-phase-1-snow.webp){: loading="lazy"}

Book both phases together in advance; you'll get a refund if it's closed. Also book the joyride prior if you wish to experience that. Carry your original ID. Two warnings. People will try to skip the queue, and fights can break out over it. And those who offer “early access” for money cannot actually do it; ignore them.

Do eat the bhel at Phase 1 before you leave. It's the perfect chatpata snack for the location.

## Day 6: Pahalgam and Pony Rides

We drove to Pahalgam and divided the journey into stages. Shopping for pashmina shawls and kashmiri kurtis. Then dry fruits, spices, kesar, and kahwa. Then a stop at an apple garden where they pressed fresh apple juice for us.

At Pahalgam we took pony rides for sightseeing. The route was on paved paths. We reached the last point around 4 pm. We were hungry, so we indulged ourselves in some bread, omelette, Maggi, and chai.

![A scenic pony ride through the beautiful mountain trails of Pahalgam, Kashmir.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/13-pony-ride-pahalgam.webp){: loading="lazy"}

At 5 pm, CRPF personnel began clearing the area for safety reasons. We took the ponies back to the car and called it a day. If you're planning Pahalgam, start early so that you don't have to rush back at 5 pm.

## Day 7: Willow Trees At Betaab Valley And Cafe Wilo

![A pony ride through the off-road forest trails of Betaab Valley, surrounded by willow trees and mountain scenery.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/14-pony-ride-betaab-valley.webp){: loading="lazy"}

We started early at Betaab Valley, which is surrounded by willow trees and a river running through the middle. The sightseeing is again through pony rides, but this one was different from the previous day. It's off-road, up and down slopes, and in between the trees. It felt like a small adventure in itself.

Our guide pointed out a spot as where the last scene of Bajrangi Bhaijaan was shot. But it's just local lore; the movie scene was shot at the base of Thajiwas Glacier near Sonmarg.

Then we headed to Cafe Wilo, which sits along the Lidder River. The outdoor riverside tables are quite in demand, so reserve your spot in advance. We did not, so we sat inside, which is just as beautiful. Order the hot chocolate without fail. Try the creamy and filling alfredo and the soft saucy pizza. And don't forget the delicious fries and chicken tenders.

![A comforting meal of creamy Alfredo pasta and hot chocolate at Cafe Wilo, overlooking the Lidder River in Pahalgam.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/15-alfredo-pasta-hot-chocolate-cafe-wilo.webp){: loading="lazy"}

After the packed 7 days, we finally headed to Katra, had a good night's sleep, and took a train back to Mumbai the next morning.

## What I Would Do Differently

### Plan The Gondola Properly

I would check the availability status the day before and plan my visit around it.

### Reserve The Table

At popular cafes like Chai Jaai and Cafe Wilo, make sure to reserve tables and visit them early in the day for the best experience.

### Reverse The Itinerary

Keep Srinagar as the last location in the trip because it's the main city where you get to go shopping. Having Srinagar as one of the first stops means you have to carry extra luggage for the whole trip.

### Keep One Buffer Day

We had a strict itinerary, which meant every long drive had to be perfect so that we could do all the activities.

## What Late Autumn Actually Felt Like

![A picturesque Kashmir road lined with trees displaying varying shades of green, yellow, orange, and golden autumn leaves.]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/images/7-days-in-kashmir/16-autumn-road-kashmir.webp){: loading="lazy"}

Kashmir at the start of November is not the Kashmir you see on posters. The chinar leaves have been shed, and the trees are bare. The light goes out quicker, and the wind is harsh. The rivers have a weak current and the mountains are waiting to turn white.

But that's the point. Harud, the Kashmiri word for autumn, starts when chinar turns orange and lasts till every leaf has fallen. We caught the end of it with snow already arriving at the peaks.

Srinagar’s temperature in November ranges from 15°C by day to 1°C at night. So pack clothes that survive the night.

They say Kashmir needs to be experienced in every season. And autumn was just the beginning for us.

## FAQ

### Is seven days enough for Kashmir?

Seven days are enough if you plan right. If you're trying to cover everything in 7 days like we did, it'll be hectic. We moved to a new location every day and still missed a lot.

### Is November a good time to visit Kashmir?

If you want to experience proper autumn, go during early to mid-October. If you want the bare trees and light snowfall, late November works.

### Do you need a permit for Sonmarg, Gulmarg, or Pahalgam?

As an Indian tourist, no, you don't need permits. You will just have to pass through security checks.

### What should vegetarians eat?

Though a lot of meat specialties are consumed in Kashmir, there are vegetarian options too. The bakeries, cafes, and local food shops are reliable for vegetarians.
